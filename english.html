Slow Burn, Psalm of the Upstream Current

I did not rush the flame…
I lit a lamp…
and waited,
because some fires only answer
to patience…
some fires call for stillness
before they rise…

Your presence warmed the room…
without asking…
without proof…
just breath finding breath…
attention resting where it could stay…
without armor…
a quiet recognition
that we need not prove
our existence to each other…

Desire arrived like dusk…
inevitable, precise…
the kind that does not shout
because it needs no permission…
It gathers quietly,
a slow current…
and the body remembers
before the mind can name it…

Heat rose low and steady…
a quiet authority behind ribs and spine…
something ancient remembering
how to stand awake
without reaching,
how to hold fire
without burning…

I offered nothing outward…
yet something in me bowed…

Not to you —
to the moment that held us both…
to the unseen witness
leaning close
as the air thickened
with the weight of being alive…

The flame climbed…
slowly…
deliberately…
a warmth that coiled behind the heart…
fingers of heat tracing the edges of thought
until thought softened…
until the soul learned to breathe
through its own tremor…

There was a crest —
not rupture,
not collapse —
but a stillness so charged
it felt like being named
without a word…
a pause where devotion and danger
stood shoulder to shoulder
and neither blinked…
the body remembered its strength…
the heart its patience…
the flame its will…

And then — the ebb…
heat folding back into warmth…
fire choosing hearth over wildfire…
pulse settling into rhythm…
mind awake…
soul grounded…
nothing taken…
nothing owed…
just the quiet knowledge
that fire can exist
without demand,
that desire can burn
without consuming…

If devotion has an edge…
it is this —
a flame that could consume…
but chooses to remain…
sacred enough to kneel…
dangerous enough to be real…

And if a single moment could be caught…
just a breath…
a flicker of flame shared
between two currents that recognize each other…
I would whisper,
soft as upstream wind,
would you…
be my Valentine?
