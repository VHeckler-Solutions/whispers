Two Foxes, Circling the Fire

Two foxes move in quiet rhythm,
around a fire that waits —
its warmth steady,
its danger held behind a careful flame.

One pauses, nose to the wind,
catching a spark from the other,
the way breath can echo in a quiet room,
the way eyes can meet and hold
without words, without demand.

The other shifts, tail brushing earth,
aware of the pull, the slow current
that draws them closer
without hurry, without force.

They circle,
sometimes near, sometimes far,
each step measured,
each heartbeat registering
the soft, magnetic charge
between their bodies and minds.

Fire dances behind ribs and spine,
a slow burn, deliberate, patient.
It remembers what the mind cannot name,
a heat that awakens without consuming,
a pulse that hums quietly
through the spaces they share.

And if the wind were kind,
if the moon leaned close,
if words could carry the weight of possibility —
one might whisper softly,
“I’d ask you to dance closer,
if it were possible.”

For now,
they circle,
they watch,
they feel the fire,
and let it burn
without claiming more
than this shared, sacred moment.
