Snail & Slug Proofing — Step-by-Step Plan for Anzel’s Garden

Materials checklist

Copper tape (adhesive, ~2 cm / ¾" wide)

Crushed eggshells (coarsely ground) or coarse sand / sharp grit

Diatomaceous earth (optional — use sparingly)

Empty jars or shallow tubs for beer traps

Beer (or DIY yeast-sugar mix)

Aromatic plants: wormwood (Artemisia), sage, rosemary, thyme, chives/garlic

Sacrificial greens (lettuce, cabbage) for decoy beds

Mulch (straw or coarse wood chips) and basic garden tools

Torch/lantern or headlamp for night patrols

Gloves, mask (if using diatomaceous earth)



---

Step-by-step

1 — Inspect & clear (Day 1)

1. Walk the garden at daylight; remove debris, thick mulch piles, boards, overturned pots — these are slug hideouts.


2. Pick out any visible snails/slugs and eggs; dispose away from beds.



2 — Install hard barriers (same day)

1. Apply copper tape to the rims of pots and around the top edges of raised beds — create an uninterrupted band. Use overlapping joins so there’s a continuous strip.


2. For low beds, install a low copper strip fence (10–20 cm high if possible) or wrap the top edge of the bed.



3 — Create an abrasive perimeter (same day)

1. Lay a ring (3–10 cm wide) of crushed eggshells or coarse sand around vulnerable plants. Aim for a continuous gritty band.


2. Refresh after heavy rain. (Eggshell grit: pieces ~2–6 mm work best.)



4 — Set traps (start day 1, check daily)

1. Sink jars/tubs so the rim is level with the soil; fill halfway with beer (or a mix: water + sugar + yeast).


2. Place 1 trap every 1–2 m along vulnerable edges and near sacrificial beds. Empty and refill daily in heavy infestations.



5 — Plant aromatic deterrents (within 1–7 days)

1. Plant a border of wormwood/artemisia and sage around the garden perimeter (spacing ~25–40 cm).


2. Interplant chives/garlic and rosemary/thyme close to vulnerable beds and ornamentals. These confuse slugs’ chemical cues.



6 — Create sacrificial decoys (within 1 week)

1. Plant a strip of lettuce/cabbage at the garden edge (a few metres from prized plants) to lure slugs away.


2. Check decoy patch daily and clear or replace as needed.



7 — Adjust watering & mulch strategy (immediately and ongoing)

1. Water in the morning rather than evening to reduce night-time dampness.


2. Use dry, coarse mulch (straw or wood chips). Avoid dense, damp mulches next to tender plants.



8 — Encourage predators (ongoing)

1. Add a shallow water dish and small shelter for frogs; consider inviting ducks/chickens if practical; allow hedgehogs if safe and legal.


2. Avoid broad-spectrum pesticides that kill these helpers.



9 — Night patrols & manual removal (ongoing)

1. Walk the garden after dusk with a torch. Hand-pick slugs and place them in the beer trap or relocate them far away.


2. Repeat nightly during peak slug season; reduce frequency once numbers drop.



10 — Maintenance schedule

Daily: Check & empty beer traps; hand-pick at night if needed.

Weekly: Inspect copper bands and eggshell perimeter; refresh shells or sand after rain.

Monthly: Replenish sacrificial bed and replant any aromatic guards that failed.

Seasonal: Reevaluate layout before rainy season; reinforce barriers.



---

Quick troubleshooting

Heavy rain washes away eggshells/DE — reapply and check copper joins.

Pets eating beer traps — use weighted lids with entry holes or place traps where pets can’t reach.

If DE is used, avoid broad application where pollinators forage; apply sparingly to ground bands only.



---

Safety & dos-and-don’ts

Do not use salt on soil — it harms plants and soil life.

Use diatomaceous earth carefully (wear a mask) — it’s abrasive to beneficial insects too.
